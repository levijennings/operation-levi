A full charter day with Ninilchik Charters. Meet the boat before dawn, run about an hour out, and anchor over a halibut hole in 100–250 ft of water. The deckhand rigs heavy conventional rods with bait and a pound of lead; you drop to the bottom, bounce, and wait for the pull. Halibut fight like a barn door. Limit is generally two each, one under 28" and one any size (guide confirms). Heated cabin, bathroom aboard. Crew fillets your fish at the dock mid-afternoon. Launch is either Ninilchik beach, 15 minutes away, where a tractor backs the trailered boat into the surf with you aboard, or Homer harbor, 45 minutes south.

A guided drift-boat day for coho. The guide picks the river the night before (Kasilof: smaller, closer; Kenai: bigger, bluer). Launch at first light; the guide rows you down through runs and holding water, anchoring or pulling over so you fish from the boat or wade the gravel bars. Mid-September silvers are fresh and aggressive, sitting in slow seams and behind structure. Most guides have you strip bright streamers (pink, chartreuse, purple) on a sink tip; some swing, some dead-drift egg patterns. Strip-set, not a trout set. Two per day. Your rod, your flies, their boat. Say today if you want both rivers this week.

A different river day. Instead of salmon you're after the Kenai's wild rainbow trout and Dolly Varden, which in September gorge on loose eggs and rotting flesh behind spawning sockeye. The technique is dead-drifting a plastic bead (an egg imitation) or a flesh fly under an indicator through the soft water below spawning fish, mending to keep it dead, setting on any hesitation. Fall Kenai rainbows run 20–30 inches and are among the largest wild trout anywhere. Catch and release only, barbless, fish kept in the water for photos. If trout are slow the guide pivots to silvers, which is why the 10wt rides along. Tomorrow you're out of the cottage by 5 am for a 10:23 flight, so tonight is pack-up night: car loaded except the toothbrush.
